The Problem

Egypt generates approximately 76,000 tons of municipal solid waste every single day. Less than 15% is formally recycled. The rest is landfilled, burned, or absorbed by informal networks that operate entirely outside any data or accountability system.

The gap is not a lack of recyclable material — it is a structural disconnect between three actors who never effectively communicate: households that generate waste, collection services that move it, and recycling companies that need it sorted and consistent. Households don't separate waste at source because they see no direct benefit, have no reliable infrastructure to receive what they separate, and receive no guidance on how to do it correctly.

The problem is behavioral and infrastructural — not material. Egypt doesn't lack recyclable waste. It lacks a coordination layer that makes recycling the path of least resistance rather than the path of most effort.

The Solution

EcoLoop is a coordination layer — not another consumer app. Rather than acquiring users one household at a time, EcoLoop enters through property managers of residential compounds as a B2B entry point. One decision from one manager covers 80–200+ units. This creates a structured, predictable supply chain for recycling companies that currently depend on inconsistent informal networks.

01
Compound Entry
Property managers sign up as the B2B customer. EcoLoop installs smart recycling stations in strategic locations across the compound — one station per 80–120 units.
B2B — Zero Consumer Friction
02
Structured Collection
Residents separate waste into color-coded compartments. IoT fill-level sensors notify verified collection partners when stations are ready. Routes are optimized automatically.
Smart Infrastructure
03
Data as Product
Every collection is logged: compound, date, material type, weight. Recycling companies pay for reliable, sortable supply with advance visibility — the data makes that possible.
Revenue from B2B Data

Competitive Landscape

Every existing solution in Egypt and the MENA region solves one side of the equation. None of them solve the coordination problem between all three actors simultaneously — and none enter through the compound manager as a B2B aggregation point.

Player Market Model What They Solve The Gap
Bekia Egypt B2C Individual-to-collector connection, cash rewards No B2B aggregation
Big Scrappers Egypt B2C + B2B Home pickup, focus on metals & aluminium No compound strategy
Drop Me Egypt Hardware Recycling vending machines, real-time tracking High capex, slow scale
Plstka Egypt B2C App Gamification, behavioral change No supply chain
Sparklo MENA Hardware AI-powered reverse vending machines Infrastructure-heavy
EcoLoop Egypt B2B2C Compound-level aggregation + smart stations + recycler supply chain Full coordination layer

MVP Scope

The MVP tests one hypothesis: if a property manager can request a scheduled recyclable waste collection through a simple digital interface, and a verified recycling partner shows up reliably, will compounds adopt the behavior consistently? Everything else is deferred until that question is answered.

01
Web App — Not Native
Two portals only: Compound Manager (request, schedule, report) and Recycling Partner (view, confirm, log). No consumer registration. No App Store dependency.
One City Only — Cairo or Alexandria
02
3–5 Verified Partners
Manually onboard licensed recycling companies. Human coordinator manages matching in Phase 1. Algorithm comes later, after behavior is proven.

03
90-Day Target
5–10 compounds onboarded. 20+ collections completed. One metric that matters: at least 70% of compound managers request a second collection.

Lessons from 2 Years

The coordination problem is harder than the tech problem
Every time I tried to build the app, I hit the same wall: who collects the waste, and how do recycling companies get consistent supply? The platform is only useful if the logistics work first. I spent two years solving the wrong layer.
B2B entry point unlocks everything
The insight that changed the model: stop trying to change individual behavior at scale. Enter through the compound manager. One decision covers hundreds of households. That's when the unit economics started making sense.
Academic institutions are not startup incubators
Two years of trying to get institutional support taught me that universities optimize for papers and presentations, not pilots. The work that moved EcoLoop forward happened outside the curriculum entirely.
A complete concept is a real deliverable
Documentation, product design, competitive research, and MVP scoping — these are not preparation for real work. They are the work. EcoLoop is not launched, but it is not an idea either. It is a startup waiting for its first partner.
